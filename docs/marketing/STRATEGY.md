# Relay.cafe — Marketing Strategy Document (v1)

## 1. Core Positioning

Relay.cafe is NOT:

* A social network
* A messaging app
* A mental health platform
* A confession app
* A chat app

Relay.cafe IS:

> A daily anonymous signal between strangers.
> A quiet ritual.
> A minimalist social experiment.

If marketing ever shifts tone away from that, it will damage trust.

---

## 2. Target Audience

Not everyone.

Specifically:

### Primary

* Privacy-minded users
* Minimalism community
* Indie hackers
* Designers who like calm tools
* People tired of algorithmic feeds
* “Early internet” nostalgia crowd

### Secondary

* Writers / journalers
* Psychology-curious users
* People who like anonymous apps (but not chaos)
* Digital detox audience

Not:

* Teen TikTok audience
* Meme communities
* High engagement social users

This is important.

---

## 3. Emotional Hook

Relay.cafe sells a feeling, not a feature.

That feeling is:

* Release without feedback
* Being witnessed once
* Impermanence
* Quiet connection

Marketing copy must reflect that.

Never lead with:

* Encryption
* KMS
* Architecture
* Features
* Tech stack

Lead with the ritual.

---

## 4. Launch Strategy (Phased)

### Phase 0 — Soft Pre-Launch (Now)

Goals:

* Quiet awareness
* No pressure
* No big promise

Actions:

* Landing page live
* One post announcing concept
* Maybe 1–2 thoughtful threads explaining philosophy

No:

* Countdown
* Waitlist
* Ads
* Influencer outreach

---

### Phase 1 — App Store Approval

When live:

Post once:

> Relay.cafe is live.
> Once a day, you can send a message to a stranger.
> It disappears after 24 hours.
> No profiles. No replies.
> Just signal.

Then stop.

Let curiosity work.

---

### Phase 2 — Community Seeding (Weeks 1–4)

Post in:

* Indie hacker communities
* Privacy forums
* Minimalist tech communities
* Hacker News (carefully)
* Reddit (thoughtfully, not spammy)

Tone:
Explain why you built it.

Not:
“Download my app.”

But:
“I built something small.”

---

### Phase 3 — Slow Organic Growth

Observe:

* How many daily users?
* Do people share screenshots?
* What kind of messages appear?
* Is there confusion?

Refine.

Then maybe:

* Write a blog post on the philosophy.
* Share architecture decisions.
* Discuss time-based design.

But only after real usage.

---

## 5. What NOT To Do

Do NOT:

* Add gamification to drive growth.
* Add push notifications to boost engagement.
* Add streak tracking.
* Add “invite friends.”
* Add social sharing prompts.
* Post daily reminders on social.
* Run paid ads early.

Relay.cafe must grow slowly.

Fast growth would destroy it.

---

## 6. Metrics That Matter

Do NOT obsess over:

* Total installs
* Vanity downloads
* Social impressions

Instead track:

* Daily Active Users (DAU)
* Send rate vs Receive rate
* Message pool health
* TTL expiration ratios
* Decryption failure count (technical health)

Your goal is:
~2000 steady daily users.

Not millions.

---

## 7. Risks & How to Mitigate

### Risk 1 — Too Quiet, No Users

Mitigation:

* Post in communities that value minimalism.
* Explain it as an experiment.
* Invite feedback.

---

### Risk 2 — Spam Invasion

Mitigation:

* Rate limiting.
* Monitor abuse patterns.
* Escalate friction if needed.

---

### Risk 3 — Viral Spike

If it suddenly spikes:

Do NOT:

* Change product.
* Add features.

Let it stabilize.

---

## 8. Long-Term Positioning

Relay.cafe should feel like:

* A place that simply exists.
* Not chasing attention.
* Not chasing funding.
* Not chasing virality.

Over time, that becomes its brand.

---

## 9. Brand Voice Guidelines

All public writing must be:

* Calm
* Precise
* Minimal
* Slightly poetic
* Not dramatic
* Not exaggerated
* Not sales-driven

Avoid:

* “Revolutionary”
* “Change the way you connect”
* “Breakthrough”
* “Disrupting social media”

Keep it grounded.

---

## 10. Founder Behavior Matters

Because this is philosophical:

Your tone publicly shapes perception.

If you behave like:

* A hype founder

The product will feel inconsistent.

If you behave like:

* A quiet builder

The product feels coherent.

---

# Final Thought

Relay.cafe will not grow because of ads.

It will grow because someone uses it,
feels something,
and quietly tells someone else.

That’s slower.
But stronger.

# 2️⃣ Hacker News / Indie Hacker Style Post

This requires a different tone.
Less poetic.
More thoughtful.
More transparent.

Here’s a draft you can use for Hacker News (Show HN format):

---

**Title:**
Show HN: Relay.cafe — one anonymous message per day, deleted after 24 hours

**Body:**

I built a small experiment called Relay.cafe.

The idea is simple:

Once a day, you can send a message to a random person somewhere in the world.
Once a day, you can receive one.

Messages exist for 24 hours.
They are never stored after that.
There are no profiles, no replies, and no history.

I wanted to see what happens when you remove feedback loops and permanence from communication.

There’s no way to know if your message was read.

It’s intentionally minimal.

Would appreciate thoughts.

---

Notice the difference:

* Less poetic.
* More architectural.
* Slightly intellectual.
* Invites discussion.
